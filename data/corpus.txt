Photosynthesis is the biological process by which green plants convert light energy into chemical energy.
During photosynthesis, chlorophyll absorbs sunlight to transform carbon dioxide and water into glucose and oxygen.
Cellular respiration breaks down glucose molecules to release usable energy in the form of adenosine triphosphate.
All living organisms rely on cellular respiration to power essential metabolic functions inside their cells.
Mitosis is a type of cell division that produces two genetically identical daughter cells for growth and repair.
Meiosis reduces the chromosome number by half to produce four genetically diverse gametes for sexual reproduction.
Deoxyribonucleic acid carries the genetic instructions required for the development and functioning of all living organisms.
Each nucleotide in a DNA molecule consists of a phosphate group, a sugar molecule, and a nitrogenous base.
RNA polymerase transcribes genetic information from DNA into messenger RNA during the process of transcription.
Ribosomes translate messenger RNA sequences into chains of amino acids to synthesize functional proteins.
Enzymes act as biological catalysts that lower activation energy and accelerate chemical reactions inside living cells.
Changes in temperature or pH can denature an enzyme and alter the shape of its active site.
The cell membrane regulates the transport of nutrients and waste products into and out of the cell.
Osmosis is the passive diffusion of water molecules across a selectively permeable membrane toward higher solute concentration.
Active transport requires cellular energy to move ions and molecules against their concentration gradient.
Ecosystems consist of biotic communities interacting with abiotic factors such as sunlight, water, soil, and temperature.
Producers capture solar energy through photosynthesis and form the base of every terrestrial and aquatic food web.
Primary consumers feed directly on producers while secondary consumers obtain energy by eating herbivores.
Decomposers break down dead organic matter and recycle essential nutrients back into the soil and atmosphere.
Natural selection favors individuals with advantageous traits that improve survival and reproductive success in a given environment.
Over many generations, natural selection drives evolutionary adaptation and the emergence of new species.
Biodiversity strengthens ecosystem resilience and helps biological communities recover from environmental disturbances.
The nervous system transmits rapid electrical and chemical signals between the brain, spinal cord, and peripheral tissues.
Neurons communicate across synapses by releasing neurotransmitters that bind to receptors on adjacent target cells.
Homeostasis maintains stable internal conditions such as body temperature, blood pH, and glucose levels.
The circulatory system transports oxygen, nutrients, hormones, and immune cells throughout the entire body.
Red blood cells contain hemoglobin, a specialized protein that binds oxygen in the lungs and delivers it to tissues.
White blood cells defend the body against pathogens by identifying and neutralizing bacteria, viruses, and foreign particles.
Vaccines stimulate the adaptive immune system to produce memory cells that provide long-term protection against infectious diseases.
Carbon cycles continuously through the atmosphere, oceans, soil, and living organisms via photosynthesis and respiration.
Nitrogen fixation converts atmospheric nitrogen gas into ammonia that plants can absorb to build proteins and nucleic acids.
Plate tectonics explains how Earth's lithosphere is divided into moving plates that shape continents, mountains, and ocean basins.
Earthquakes occur when accumulated stress along geological fault lines is suddenly released as seismic waves.
Volcanic eruptions bring molten magma, gases, and ash from Earth's mantle to the planetary surface.
Weathering breaks down surface rocks into smaller mineral particles through physical, chemical, and biological processes.
Erosion transports weathered sediment across landscapes through the action of wind, flowing water, glaciers, and gravity.
The water cycle circulates water between oceans, atmosphere, and land through evaporation, condensation, and precipitation.
Greenhouse gases trap outgoing infrared radiation in the atmosphere and regulate global surface temperatures.
Burning fossil fuels increases atmospheric carbon dioxide concentrations and accelerates global climate change.
Renewable energy sources such as solar, wind, and hydroelectric power generate electricity without releasing carbon emissions.
Newton's first law of motion states that an object remains at rest or in uniform motion unless acted upon by an external force.
Newton's second law establishes that the acceleration of an object is directly proportional to the net force and inversely proportional to its mass.
Newton's third law states that for every action force there is an equal and opposite reaction force.
Kinetic energy depends on both the mass of a moving object and the square of its velocity.
Potential energy is stored energy resulting from an object's position in a gravitational field or elastic deformation.
The law of conservation of energy states that energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed from one form to another.
Electric current is the flow of electric charge through a conductor driven by a difference in electric potential.
Ohm's law relates voltage, current, and resistance through the fundamental equation voltage equals current times resistance.
Magnetic fields are produced by moving electric charges and exert forces on other moving charges and magnetic materials.
Electromagnetic waves carry energy through space at the speed of light without requiring a physical medium.
Sound waves are longitudinal mechanical vibrations that travel through solids, liquids, and gases at different speeds.
Light exhibits both wave-like interference and particle-like photon behavior depending on how it is observed.
Refraction occurs when a light wave changes speed and bends as it passes from one transparent medium into another.
Atoms consist of a dense positively charged nucleus surrounded by negatively charged electrons in quantized energy levels.
Covalent bonds form when two atoms share pairs of valence electrons to achieve a stable electron configuration.
Ionic bonds result from the electrostatic attraction between positively charged cations and negatively charged anions.
Chemical reactions rearrange atoms into new substances while conserving total mass and atomic count.
Acids release hydrogen ions in aqueous solution whereas bases accept hydrogen ions or release hydroxide ions.
The periodic table organizes chemical elements by increasing atomic number and recurring patterns in chemical properties.
Scientific inquiry relies on testable hypotheses, controlled experiments, empirical measurement, and peer review.
